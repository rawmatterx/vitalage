## Verdict

Activity measures whose held-out C-index change has a 95% CI above zero: PhenoAge + log censored steps (CV), PhenoAge + log counts/min, PhenoAge + log raw steps (CV), PhenoAge + log(1 + MVPA min/day).

**The combined VitalAge (PhenoAge + daily steps) beats PhenoAge alone on held-out data, so it is shipped as `phenoage/combined.py`.** The gain is modest: +0.006 in C-index.

## The combined VitalAge model

Fitted on all 2885 people in 2005-06 with steps. It maps PhenoAge and censored hip steps to one age in years with Levine's method: the age whose 10-year mortality in an age-only Gompertz model equals the person's 10-year mortality in the combined model. Because that mapping is linear in the model's linear predictor, VitalAge splits exactly into years per input:

- PhenoAge enters as acceleration: PhenoAge minus (-7.156 + 1.060 x age), its trend on age in this sample, limited to -12.5 to +29.4 years (1st and 99th percentiles);
- each calendar year adds **0.872 years** of VitalAge, each year of PhenoAge acceleration adds **0.425 years**;
- each doubling of daily steps changes VitalAge by **-3.84 years** at the same PhenoAge;
- reference: median censored hip steps 5785/day.

In-sample C-index (optimistic): VitalAge 0.866, PhenoAge 0.857. Mean VitalAge 48.6 vs mean age 49.7.

## How band values map to the NHANES inputs

- **Steps.** The model uses hip ActiGraph 7164 steps counted only in minutes with >= 500 counts (censored), because the 7164's raw step function counts many spurious steps during light movement (Tudor-Locke 2009: 9,676 raw vs 6,540 censored steps/day for US adults). Wrist bands count more steps than hip ActiGraphs: about +1,400 steps/day for wrist Fitbits vs a hip GT3X in free living, with 95% limits of agreement of roughly -2,300 to +5,200 (Fitbit Charge 2 vs GT3X, PLoS One 2020; Fitbit Flex vs wGT3X: median +1,300, MAPE 15.5%). VitalAge therefore converts band steps as `hip_steps = band_steps - 1400` (never below 500). This chain is uncertain: the comparison studies used the newer GT3X and raw steps, not the 7164's censored steps, and band algorithms differ by brand. Across the limits of agreement, the hip-equivalent of 8,000 band steps ranges from about 3000 to 10300, which moves VitalAge by about 6.8 years.
- **MVPA minutes.** Hip counts >= 2020/min have no reliable equivalent in band "active minutes", which use heart rate or proprietary cadence rules, so MVPA is reported here for research but not used in VitalAge.
- **Counts per minute** have no band equivalent and are research-only.

## Methods

- **Sample:** NHANES 2003-2004 and 2005-2006 adults aged 20-84 with all 9 PhenoAge markers, eligible for mortality linkage, follow-up > 0 months. PhenoAge from `phenoage.core` with the published coefficients (Levine 2018).
- **Activity:** hip-worn ActiGraph 7164, 1-minute epochs, up to 7 days. Non-wear = runs of >= 60 zero-count minutes (Troiano et al. 2008, without their 2-minute allowance). Valid day >= 10 h wear; >= 4 valid days. Features: mean counts per wear minute, minutes/day >= 2020 counts/min (MVPA), and (2005-06) steps/day, raw and censored to minutes >= 500 counts (floored at 500 steps/day before taking logs).
- **Model:** Gompertz proportional hazards on months since exam, maximum likelihood. PhenoAge enters as age plus PhenoAge acceleration (residual of PhenoAge on age, winsorized at its 1st and 99th percentiles because a few very sick people have PhenoAges above 100), so activity is judged on what it adds beyond both.
- **Validation:** temporal split (fit 2003-04, test 2005-06) for counts and MVPA; 5-fold cross-validation for steps; Harrell's C-index with 300 paired bootstrap resamples for 95% CIs.

## Limitations

- Unweighted: estimates describe this sample, not the US population.
- One week of hip accelerometry at baseline; activity can change over 13-16 years of follow-up, and ill people move less (reverse causation), which inflates activity's apparent effect on early deaths.
- ActiGraph 7164 hip counts and its step function are not the same as a wrist band's step count; band steps typically run higher, so coefficients do not transfer one-to-one to consumer bands.
- US data from 2003-2006; not validated in Indian populations.
- The simplified non-wear rule may count some low-movement wear as non-wear.
