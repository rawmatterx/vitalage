## The preliminary combined VitalAge model (withdrawn)

Fitted on all 2885 people in 2005-06 with steps. It maps PhenoAge and censored hip steps to one age in years with Levine's method: the age whose 10-year mortality in an age-only Gompertz model equals the person's 10-year mortality in the combined model. Because that mapping is linear in the model's linear predictor, VitalAge splits exactly into years per input:

- PhenoAge enters as acceleration: PhenoAge minus (-7.156 + 1.060 x age), its trend on age in this sample, limited to -12.5 to +29.4 years (1st and 99th percentiles);
- each calendar year adds **0.872 years** of VitalAge, each year of PhenoAge acceleration adds **0.425 years**;
- each doubling of daily steps changes VitalAge by **-3.84 years** at the same PhenoAge;
- reference: median censored hip steps 5785/day.

In-sample C-index (optimistic): VitalAge 0.866, PhenoAge 0.857. Mean VitalAge 48.6 vs mean age 49.7.

## How band values map to the NHANES inputs

- **Steps.** The model uses hip ActiGraph 7164 steps counted only in minutes with >= 500 counts (censored), because the 7164's raw step function counts many spurious steps during light movement (Tudor-Locke 2009: 9,676 raw vs 6,540 censored steps/day for US adults). Wrist bands count more steps than hip ActiGraphs: about +1,400 steps/day for wrist Fitbits vs a hip GT3X in free living, with 95% limits of agreement of roughly -2,300 to +5,200 (Fitbit Charge 2 vs GT3X, PLoS One 2020; Fitbit Flex vs wGT3X: median +1,300, MAPE 15.5%). VitalAge therefore converts band steps as `hip_steps = band_steps - 1400` (never below 500). This chain is uncertain: the comparison studies used the newer GT3X and raw steps, not the 7164's censored steps, and band algorithms differ by brand. Across the limits of agreement, the hip-equivalent of 8,000 band steps ranges from about 3000 to 10300, which moves VitalAge by about 6.8 years.
- **MVPA minutes.** Hip counts >= 2020/min have no reliable equivalent in band "active minutes", which use heart rate or proprietary cadence rules, so MVPA is reported here for research but not used in VitalAge.
- **Counts per minute** have no band equivalent and are research-only.

### Verdict

**No combined model is shipped.** The model the pre-registered rule chose did not beat PhenoAge on the locked test set: the 95% CI of the 10-year C change includes zero. The steps model shipped earlier (D3) also fails this test. Blood age (PhenoAge) and the literature-based Fitness Age stay as they are.

Some models not chosen in CV did well on the test set (notably D7, a ridge refit of the 9 markers with steps and pulse, and the research-only refits R3/R3b). Choosing one of them now would use the test set for selection, so they are reported but not shipped. They are reasonable candidates for a future study with a fresh test set, for example NHANES 2011-2014 wrist accelerometry.

The boosted-tree ceiling (R5, a simple stump-boosted Cox model written for this study) did worse than PhenoAge in CV and on the test set, so it says little about how much signal a simple model misses; a tuned random survival forest would be a better ceiling.

### How deployable inputs map from NHANES to a wrist band

| Band input | NHANES variable used | Mapping and uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Steps | PAXSTEP, counted in minutes >= 500 counts (hip ActiGraph 7164) | Wrist bands count about 1,400 more steps/day than hip ActiGraphs, limits of agreement about -2,300 to +5,200; the studies used the newer GT3X and raw steps. High uncertainty. |
| MVPA minutes | minutes >= 2020 counts/min on the hip (Troiano 2008) | No validated mapping. Band "active minutes" use heart rate or cadence rules that differ by brand; a cadence of about 100 steps/min is a common moderate-intensity threshold, but the count cut-point and band minutes are not interchangeable. Very high uncertainty. |
| Resting heart rate | BPXPLS, 60-second seated pulse after 5 minutes' rest at the exam | Band resting HR is usually measured during sleep or long rest and runs several bpm lower than a seated clinic pulse; no conversion was fitted. Moderate uncertainty. |
| Peak 30-min cadence | mean of each day's 30 highest step-minutes (hip) | Bands can report cadence, but rarely this summary; same step-count caveats. High uncertainty. |
| Sleep duration and timing | not available: NHANES 2003-06 asked people to remove the monitor at night | Cannot be tested with these data. |

Feature definitions follow Troiano et al. 2008 (wear, MVPA), Tudor-Locke et al. 2009 (censored steps), van Someren et al. 1999 (rest-activity rhythm) and Smirnova et al. 2020, *J Gerontol A* 75(9):1779-1785, doi:10.1093/gerona/glz193 (total activity and active/sedentary transition probabilities in NHANES 2003-06).

## Methods

- **Sample:** NHANES 2003-2004 and 2005-2006 adults aged 20-84 with all 9 PhenoAge markers, eligible for mortality linkage, follow-up > 0 months. PhenoAge from `phenoage.core` with the published coefficients (Levine 2018).
- **Activity:** hip-worn ActiGraph 7164, 1-minute epochs, up to 7 days. Non-wear = runs of >= 60 zero-count minutes (Troiano et al. 2008, without their 2-minute allowance). Valid day >= 10 h wear; >= 4 valid days. Features: mean counts per wear minute, minutes/day >= 2020 counts/min (MVPA), and (2005-06) steps/day, raw and censored to minutes >= 500 counts (floored at 500 steps/day before taking logs).
- **Model:** Gompertz proportional hazards on months since exam, maximum likelihood. PhenoAge enters as age plus PhenoAge acceleration (residual of PhenoAge on age, winsorized at its 1st and 99th percentiles because a few very sick people have PhenoAges above 100), so activity is judged on what it adds beyond both.
- **Validation:** temporal split (fit 2003-04, test 2005-06) for counts and MVPA; 5-fold cross-validation for steps; Harrell's C-index with 300 paired bootstrap resamples for 95% CIs.

## Limitations

- Unweighted: estimates describe this sample, not the US population.
- One week of hip accelerometry at baseline; activity can change over 13-16 years of follow-up, and ill people move less (reverse causation), which inflates activity's apparent effect on early deaths.
- ActiGraph 7164 hip counts and its step function are not the same as a wrist band's step count; band steps typically run higher, so coefficients do not transfer one-to-one to consumer bands.
- US data from 2003-2006; not validated in Indian populations.
- The simplified non-wear rule may count some low-movement wear as non-wear.

## 5. Wrist study, NHANES 2011-2014: pre-registration

Written and committed before any 2011-2014 model was fitted or any test row
was looked at. Code: `research/wrist.py`.

- **Data.** NHANES 2011-2012 and 2013-2014 adults aged 20-79 with the 8
  available PhenoAge markers (CRP was not measured in these cycles), the
  exam pulse where present, and >= 4 valid days of 24-hour wrist ActiGraph
  GT3X+ data from the daily (PAXDAY) and hourly (PAXHR) summaries. A valid
  day is a full 1,440-minute day with >= 16 h classified as wake or sleep
  wear. Mortality: NCHS linked mortality through 31 Dec 2019.
- **Primary outcome.** 5-year all-cause mortality (follow-up truncated at 60
  months), Harrell's C, with the difference vs the blood-only baseline and a
  paired bootstrap 95% CI (200 resamples). Also reported: calibration
  (mean predicted vs Kaplan-Meier observed, calibration slope).
- **CRP.** (a) PhenoAge computed with CRP fixed at the reference value
  (1 mg/L). Because CRP enters as a constant for everyone, setting it to the
  range ends (0.2 or 10 mg/L) shifts every PhenoAge equally and cannot change
  any C-index; this is reported, not tested. (b) A ridge-penalised refit of
  the 8 markers plus age, as a second blood baseline.
- **Test set.** 30% of eligible adults, stratified by death, drawn with a
  fixed seed; the SEQNs and their SHA-256 are committed before fitting.
- **Candidates** (all add age and capped PhenoAge acceleration, except W8):
  W1 log daily MIMS volume; W2 sleep duration (linear + squared);
  W3 sleep regularity (SD of nightly sleep minutes) and sleep midpoint with
  its SD; W4 rest-activity rhythm (IS, IV, relative amplitude); W5 exam
  pulse; W6 MIMS + sleep duration + pulse; W7 all wrist features + pulse
  (ridge); W8 ridge refit of the 8 markers + all wrist features + pulse.
  Minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity cannot be computed from hourly
  summaries and are not tested.
- **Choices made only by 5-fold CV in the 70%:** the PhenoAge acceleration
  cap, ridge penalties, and the deployed model.
- **Deployment rule.** Order: W5, W1, W2, W3, W4, W6, W7, W8. Start from the
  blood baseline (age + PhenoAge acceleration); step up only if the
  out-of-fold 5-year C beats the current choice by >= 0.003 with the
  bootstrap CI above zero, on the same people.
- **Shipping rule.** A combined VitalAge ships only if the deployed model's
  5-year C gain over the blood baseline on the locked 30% has a 95% CI
  that excludes zero.
- **Amendments before locking (no model fitted yet).** Ages are 20-79, not
  20-84, because these cycles record everyone aged 80+ as 80. Sleep
  midpoint is kept on a noon-to-noon clock so that 23:50 and 00:10 are
  20 minutes apart.

### How wrist features map to consumer bands

| Feature | NHANES variable | Mapping and uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Daily activity volume | PAXMTSD, daily sum of MIMS units (wrist GT3X+) | Consumer bands do not report MIMS; they report steps, "active minutes" or proprietary scores. Open-source MIMS can be computed from raw band acceleration where a band exports it (few do). No validated conversion from band steps to MIMS. High uncertainty. |
| Sleep duration | PAXSWMD, minutes classified as sleep wear by the NHANES algorithm | Bands report sleep time from their own algorithms (often heart rate + motion); they typically differ from actigraphy by tens of minutes a night. Moderate uncertainty. |
| Sleep midpoint and regularity | from hourly sleep-wear minutes (PAXHR), noon-to-noon nights | Bands report bedtime and wake time, so midpoint and its night-to-night SD are directly computable; hourly resolution here limits precision to about 30 minutes. Moderate uncertainty. |
| Rest-activity rhythm (IS, IV, RA) | from hourly MIMS (PAXHR) | Computable from any band's hourly step or activity counts, but values depend on the activity measure used. Moderate to high uncertainty. |
| Resting heart rate | BPXPLS, 60-second seated exam pulse | Band resting HR is lower than a seated clinic pulse; no conversion fitted. Moderate uncertainty. |

Minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity could not be computed: the hourly and daily files have no minute-level intensities, and the minute files were not downloaded.

## 9. Tracking DNA age with a blood score, NHANES 1999-2002: pre-registration

Purpose (the project's goal from 2026-11-04 on): VitalAge should report how
much aging the body shows now and how fast it is moving, not forecast death.
So the yardstick here is DNA-methylation aging, not mortality. Written and
committed before any model is fitted. Code: `research/dna_age.py`.

- **Data.** NHANES 1999-2000 and 2001-2002 adults aged 50+ with the CDC's
  public DNA methylation clocks (`dnmepi.sas7bdat`) and all 9 PhenoAge blood
  markers. Exam pulse is used where present.
- **Targets.** Each is the residual of a DNA clock on calendar age (fitted in
  the training set), i.e. how much older or younger the DNA looks than the
  birthday says.
  - Primary 1, internal-age state: GrimAge2 acceleration (years).
  - Primary 2, pace of aging: DunedinPoAm (biological years per year).
  - Secondary: Horvath, Hannum and DNA PhenoAge acceleration, and telomere
    length. DNA PhenoAge was trained to predict blood PhenoAge, so agreement
    with it is partly circular.
- **Scores compared.**
  - S0: blood PhenoAge acceleration (published formula, residual on age).
  - S1: ridge regression of the target on the 9 markers (CRP as ln) + age.
  - S2: S1 + exam pulse.
- **Metric.** Pearson correlation between a score (as an age-independent
  residual) and the target residual, on the locked test set. For each
  primary target: S1 minus S0, with a paired bootstrap 95% CI (2,000
  resamples).
- **Split.** 30% test set stratified by sex and 10-year age band, SEQNs and
  SHA-256 committed before fitting. Ridge penalty chosen by 5-fold CV in the
  70%.
- **Success rule.** For a primary target, the refit "tracks DNA aging better
  than PhenoAge" if the CI of S1 minus S0 excludes zero. S2 is reported but
  carries no success rule.
- **Survey weights** are not used (unweighted), as in earlier sections.
- **Amendment before any fitting.** Ages are 50-84: these cycles record
  everyone aged 85+ as 85, so they are excluded, as in sections 5-8.

## 10. Result: a blood score tracks DNA aging better than PhenoAge

Run once on the locked test set (commit 3f93815): 716 adults aged
50-84; training 1672. Correlations are between age-independent
residuals (how much older or younger than the birthday each measure says).

| DNA target | PhenoAge (S0) | Blood refit (S1) | Refit + pulse (S2) | S1 minus S0 (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GrimAge2 acceleration (internal-age state) - primary | 0.381 | 0.492 | 0.492 | +0.111 (+0.057 to +0.164) |
| DunedinPoAm (pace of aging) - primary | 0.302 | 0.489 | 0.496 | +0.187 (+0.121 to +0.249) |
| Horvath acceleration | 0.117 | 0.173 | 0.176 | +0.056 (-0.024 to +0.133) |
| Hannum acceleration | 0.147 | 0.302 | 0.308 | +0.156 (+0.080 to +0.226) |
| DNA PhenoAge acceleration (partly circular) | 0.267 | 0.400 | 0.402 | +0.133 (+0.075 to +0.188) |
| DNA telomere length (refit predicts length; PhenoAge sign is negative = shorter) | -0.093 | 0.269 | 0.262 | +0.362 (+0.217 to +0.515) |

**Both pre-registered primary tests succeed.** The 9-marker blood refit
tracks DNA internal age (GrimAge2) and DNA pace of aging (DunedinPoAm)
clearly better than PhenoAge does, and it also beats PhenoAge for Hannum
and DNA PhenoAge. Exam pulse adds nothing.

**What drives it** (standardized ridge weights, largest first):
- GrimAge2: lymphocyte_pct -1.13, mcv +1.06, rdw +1.06, wbc +0.88, crp +0.78, glucose +0.47, alp +0.42, creatinine +0.25, albumin -0.18.
- DunedinPoAm: lymphocyte_pct -0.03, mcv +0.02, rdw +0.02, wbc +0.01, crp +0.01, alp +0.01, glucose +0.00, albumin -0.00, creatinine -0.00.

Lower lymphocyte %, higher MCV, RDW, WBC and CRP lead both. Part of this is
the blood signature of smoking, which GrimAge encodes directly (pack-years)
and which also raises DunedinPoAm. The score therefore partly measures
smoking-related aging. That is real and modifiable, but it should be stated
to users.

**What a correlation of about 0.49 means.** The blood score explains roughly
a quarter of the person-to-person variation in DNA aging beyond calendar
age. It is a meaningful marker of internal aging, not a substitute for a
DNA test.

**Limits.** US adults aged 50-84 from 1999-2002, unweighted, one time point
(so this validates the "right now" score; pace from repeat blood tests still
needs repeat-measure data). Not validated in Indian populations or under 50.
Nothing in the package or the web page has changed.
